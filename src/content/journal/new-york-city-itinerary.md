My first New York trip, I tried to see the entire city in three days and ended up exhausted, cranky, and stuck on a subway platform more than I was anywhere actually worth seeing. This is the itinerary I wish I'd had, built around neighborhoods instead of a scattered checklist.

## Day one: downtown and the classics

Start early at the 9/11 Memorial before it gets crowded, then walk the short distance to the Financial District and down to the Staten Island Ferry for a free, no-line view of the Statue of Liberty from the water. Walk the Brooklyn Bridge in the early afternoon (from Manhattan into Brooklyn, so the skyline is in front of you the whole way), reward yourself at Dumbo, and grab dinner in the neighborhood before heading back.

## Day two: midtown and the park

Central Park in the morning, before the paths fill up, ending at the Met if you have any museum stamina left. Walk down through the neighborhoods around Fifth Avenue in the afternoon, then head to the High Line an hour or so before sunset, it's genuinely better with the light low. Chelsea Market is right at the end of it for dinner, so you don't have to plan a second stop.

## Day three: the neighborhoods locals actually love

This is the day to slow down. Wander the West Village with no real agenda, get a coffee, and just walk the tree-lined streets. Cross into SoHo for the afternoon, then head downtown to the Lower East Side for dinner. Save your last evening for a rooftop bar, ideally one that isn't charging a cover just for the view.

## A few notes that saved me

- **Buy a weekly MetroCard on day one.** It pays for itself by day two and saves you from the tap-in fumbling every single ride.
- **Book timed entry for anything popular** (museums, the ferry, observation decks) the night before, not the morning of.
- **Wear shoes you've already broken in.** You will walk more than you think, no matter how much subway you plan around it.
- **Leave one block of unplanned time each day.** The best thing on my trip was a bookstore I wasn't looking for.
