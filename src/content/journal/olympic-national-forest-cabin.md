I booked this cabin on a whim after a stretch of weeks that were all noise, work Slack notifications bleeding into weekends, a phone that never stopped buzzing, and picked a spot specifically because the listing bragged about having no cell service. That should have been a warning. It was actually the entire point.

The cabin sat right on a small lake inside Olympic National Forest, one room, a wood stove, a dock just steps from the door, and exactly the kind of quiet that takes a full day to actually sink into. My first few hours there I kept reaching for my phone out of habit and finding nothing to do with it, which was its own kind of uncomfortable before it became the best part of the trip.

Mornings were coffee on the dock watching mist come off the water. Afternoons were a mix of short hikes from the cabin and doing genuinely nothing, which I am usually terrible at and somehow wasn't there. I rented a kayak from a small outfitter down the road and spent an entire afternoon paddling the shoreline, and at night, with zero light pollution, the stars were the kind you forget exist when you live in a city.

## If you're planning your own

- **Book well ahead.** Lake cabins in the area go fast, especially summer weekends.
- **Bring actual cash and a full tank of gas.** The nearest town is small and not everything takes card.
- **Pack a real flashlight, not just your phone.** There is no ambient light out there at all.
- **Download anything you need offline before you go.** That includes maps.

I left that cabin more rested than I've been from trips twice as long and twice as expensive. If you only take one recommendation from this whole blog, let it be this one.
