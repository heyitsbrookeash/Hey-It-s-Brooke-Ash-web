Charleston was a last-minute add to a trip I'd already planned around Savannah, mostly because a coworker said "if you're already down there, you have to see Charleston too," and she was right in a way that mildly annoyed me the whole drive up.

The historic district does a lot of the work for you. Rainbow Row alone is worth the walk, all those pastel houses standing shoulder to shoulder like they're posing for a photo you didn't even have to ask for. I did the horse carriage tour on a whim, expected to feel like a tourist cliché, and instead spent forty minutes getting genuinely good history from a guide who clearly loved the job. Worth it, no notes.

The Battery at golden hour is the move if you only have time for one thing. Spanish moss hanging off every tree, water on one side, mansions on the other, and enough benches that you can just sit there and let the evening happen instead of rushing through it. I also made the drive out to Angel Oak, a live oak tree that's somewhere north of four hundred years old, and stood under it for a genuinely embarrassing amount of time doing nothing but looking up.

What got me most, though, was the pace. Nobody was in a hurry. Servers chatted, strangers said good morning on the sidewalk, and I found myself walking slower without deciding to. I came home and immediately looked up flights back, which is usually a good sign a trip did its job.

I ate extremely well on this trip too, enough that it earned [its own separate post](/journal/charleston-eats).
