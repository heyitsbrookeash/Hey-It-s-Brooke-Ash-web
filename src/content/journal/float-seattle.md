I'd been curious about float tanks for years and put it off for just as long, mostly because "get into a dark pod full of salt water for ninety minutes" sounded like the setup to a horror movie, not a wellness routine. A slow month at work finally pushed me to book it, and now I'm the person who brings it up unprompted at dinner.

The tank holds about a thousand pounds of dissolved Epsom salt in ten inches of water, heated to skin temperature, so within a few minutes you genuinely can't tell where your body ends and the water starts. You can float with the light on or off, music playing or silent. I did total darkness and silence on my first try, mostly out of pride, and spent the first fifteen minutes doing anything but relaxing (my brain apparently had several years of unfiled thoughts to get through first).

Somewhere around the halfway mark it flips. Time gets weird, my shoulders finally dropped from wherever they permanently live near my ears, and I stopped tracking anything at all until the lights slowly came back up to signal it was over. I walked out loose in a way I don't think I've been since before I had a commute.

## What I'd tell a first-timer

- **Don't shave or wax the day of.** Salt water and fresh skin are not friends.
- **Skip caffeine beforehand** if you actually want to relax instead of narrating your to-do list for an hour.
- **The first ten minutes feel weird.** Everyone says this and everyone is right. Stay in it.
- **Book the shower time after seriously.** You will be salty in places you didn't know could get salty.

I'm not going to pretend this replaces therapy or a full night of sleep. But as a once-a-month reset button, it's earned a permanent spot in my routine, right alongside [the sauna and cold plunge routine](/journal/wild-haus-sauna) for the days I want the reset to feel a little more intense.
